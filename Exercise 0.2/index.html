Energy Consumption in Australian Homes

Household appliances are an important part of everyday life in Australia. Refrigerators, televisions, washing machines, air conditioners and other electrical appliances all contribute to household electricity consumption.

The amount of energy an appliance uses depends on several factors, including its power rating, how long it is used, its age, its efficiency and the way it is operated.

Learning about appliance energy consumption can help households identify opportunities to reduce unnecessary electricity use and potentially lower their electricity bills.

Simple Ways to Reduce Energy Consumption

Turn Appliances Off

Switch appliances off when they are not being used instead of leaving them running unnecessarily.

Use Efficient Appliances

Energy-efficient appliances can help reduce the amount of electricity required for everyday tasks.

Reduce Standby Power

Turning appliances completely off can reduce the electricity they consume while in standby mode.

Frequently Asked Questions

Appliance energy consumption refers to the amount of electrical energy an appliance uses while operating. This can vary depending on the appliance's power rating and how long it is used.

Larger appliances such as air conditioners, electric heaters, refrigerators and clothes dryers can consume significant amounts of electricity. Actual consumption depends on the appliance and how frequently it is used.

Some televisions continue to consume a small amount of electricity while in standby mode. Completely switching off the television can reduce standby electricity consumption.

Using appliances only when needed, selecting efficient appliances, reducing unnecessary standby use and following manufacturer energy recommendations can help reduce electricity consumption.
