Television Energy Consumption

Learn about the factors that influence television electricity consumption.

How Much Energy Does a Television Use?

Televisions consume electricity whenever they are operating. The amount of electricity used depends on factors such as screen size, display technology, brightness settings, operating time and the television's energy efficiency.

A television that is used for several hours every day can contribute to household electricity consumption over time. Understanding these factors can help users make more informed choices about television use.

Television Technologies

LED Televisions

LED televisions use an LED-backlit LCD display. They are widely available and can provide relatively efficient operation depending on the model and settings.

OLED Televisions

OLED televisions use individual pixels that can produce their own light. Their energy consumption can vary depending on the content displayed and the brightness level.

QLED Televisions

QLED televisions are based on LCD technology and use quantum-dot technology to enhance colour and brightness. Energy use varies between models.

Factors Affecting Television Energy Use

1

Screen Size

Larger screens can require more electricity, although actual consumption varies between models.

2

Brightness

Higher brightness settings can increase the electricity used by some televisions.

3

Usage Time

A television that operates for more hours generally consumes more electricity.

4

Energy Efficiency

Different television models can have different energy efficiency characteristics.

Tips for Reducing Television Energy Use

  • Turn the television off when nobody is watching.
  • Avoid unnecessarily high brightness settings.
  • Use available energy-saving modes.
  • Consider the television's energy rating when purchasing a new model.
  • Reduce unnecessary standby operation where practical.
